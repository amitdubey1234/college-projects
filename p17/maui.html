Hawaii, USA

Celebrated as the 'Valley Isle', Maui unites soaring volcanic summits, pristine rainforest valleys, and sun-drenched golden beaches.
From winding coastal highways to thriving whale-nursery channels, it offers an unmatched blend of Hawaiian heritage and wild adventure.

Adventure

  • Stargazing and watching the sunrise from the 10,023-foot summit crater of Haleakalā, followed by downhill alpine biking
  • Navigating the legendary 64-mile Road to Hana through 620 hairpin curves, cascading waterfalls, and bamboo rainforests
  • Winter catamaran charters through the Auau Channel to witness humpback whale pods nursing their newborn calves
  • Snorkeling inside the submerged volcanic crescent crater of Molokini with endemic Hawaiian green sea turtles (honu)
  • Windsurfing and kitesurfing along the world-renowned ocean swells of Ho'okipa Beach on the rugged north shore

Cuisine

  • Authentic Hawaiian Ahi Poke: freshly diced yellowfin tuna tossed with toasted sesame oil, tamari, scallions, and Hawaiian ogo sea salt
  • Kalua Pork: succulent, smoky slow-roasted pork shoulder cooked inside an underground imu with kiawe hardwood and banana leaves
  • Seared Opakapaka (Pink Snapper): wild island catch served over coconut jasmine rice with fresh ginger-lilikoi emulsion
  • Poi & Lomi Salmon: traditional fermented taro root mash served alongside salt-cured salmon, crushed ice, and sweet Maui sweet onions
  • Shave ice layered with sweetened condensed milk, tropical passion fruit (lilikoi) syrups, and warm malasada doughnuts

Facts

  • Haleakalā Crater is large enough to comfortably fit the island of Manhattan inside its colorful cinder-cone basin
  • The shallow Auau Channel between Maui and Lanai represents one of the dense winter humpback whale sanctuaries on Earth
  • Lahaina served as the royal capital of the Kingdom of Hawaii from 1820 to 1845 under King Kamehameha III
  • Historically served as the primary Pacific center for the global whaling fleet and later the commercial Hawaiian sugar cane empire
  • Home to the rare silversword plant (Ahinahina), an evolutionary marvel found nowhere else on Earth that thrives on barren volcanic cinder
  • Maui was named after the legendary demigod Maui, who according to Polynesian mythology lassoed the sun to slow its journey across the sky
